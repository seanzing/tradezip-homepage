A five-minute briefing for roofing owners

Six roofers were called. One showed up.

Guess who got the job? Plus, why a quiet hurricane map is not permission to nap, the latest roofing price signal and a $282,420 safety lesson.

THE SHORT VERSION: Speed wins. Proof beats promises. Safety shortcuts are ruinously expensive.
1 The big thing

The easiest roofing sale in America is apparently answering the phone.

A homeowner recently described calling six local roofing companies about a replacement. Two returned the call. Only one actually arrived and supplied a quote. That company completed the roof.

There was no complicated closing technique. The winner answered, arrived and made it easy to buy. That should be slightly embarrassing for the industry and extremely encouraging for any roofing company willing to run a tight front office.

The winning sales strategy was not being the cheapest. It was being the only actual option.

Steal this: the 10-minute callback stack

For every missed new-business call
  • Immediately: Send a text confirming you received the request.
  • Within 10 minutes: Call back, even if you are calling from the truck.
  • If they miss you: Text two specific inspection windows.
  • Next morning: Send one final, polite follow-up with a booking link.
Copy this text Hi [Name], this is [Name] from [Roofing Company]. I saw your request and can help. I have inspection openings Tuesday at 3:30 or Wednesday at 9:00. Which works better?
2 Weather board

The Atlantic is quiet. Your storm plan should not be.

NOAA's August update gives the 2026 Atlantic season a 75% chance of finishing below normal. That sounds like permission to relax, until you reach the important part: most of the season's expected activity is still likely during August, September and October.

7 to 13named storms forecast for the full season
2 to 6could become hurricanes
0 to 2could become major hurricanes

NOAA is also explicit that its seasonal forecast is not a landfall forecast. A quiet basin can still produce one damaging event. As of August 25, forecasters were monitoring two Atlantic systems, with no Florida effects expected during the following week.

Do this before there is a named storm on the map
  • Prewrite your emergency tarp, inspection and storm-damage pages for the counties you genuinely serve.
  • Confirm your after-hours phone routing and the person who owns every new lead.
  • Prepare a photo checklist so every inspection produces consistent, usable evidence.
  • Build a one-page homeowner guide explaining your process without promising an insurance outcome.
3 Safety note

$282,420 is a brutal way to learn what a five-minute check could catch.

Cal/OSHA recently announced proposed penalties totaling $282,420 against three roofing contractors after a worker fell about 24 feet and died. Inspectors reported missing fall protection, insufficient ladder-safety training, no on-site worker certified in first aid and heat-training failures involving two employers.

$282,420
Proposed penalties in one California case.
The human cost was far worse. The worker's death was described by Cal/OSHA as entirely preventable.

Federal OSHA generally requires fall protection for residential construction work six feet or more above a lower level. State rules can be more specific. This is not paperwork for paperwork's sake. It is the part of the morning that makes sure everyone comes home.

Tomorrow morning's tailgate check
  • Who inspected today's anchors, harnesses, ropes and ladder setup?
  • Who is the on-site first-aid person?
  • Does every new or returning worker know today's fall and heat plan?
  • Where are the water, shade and emergency details?
  • Who records that the check and training happened?

Safety requirements vary by job and jurisdiction. Use the applicable federal and state standards and competent safety advice for your operation.

4 Price watch

If your old estimate template is still open, close it.

The July Producer Price Index showed prepared asphalt, tar roofing and siding products up 3.6% from a year earlier. Major distributors have also posted multiple 2026 residential, freight and commercial price-increase notices.

+3.6% Year-over-year change in the July 2026 producer-price index for prepared asphalt, tar roofing and siding products.

That does not mean every bundle or supplier moved by exactly 3.6%. It means an estimate built from old costs is not a harmless admin mistake. It is a margin leak wearing a PDF.

Three lines every quote should make obvious
  • The date the price expires.
  • What can change if material or freight costs move before ordering.
  • Whether the price locks at acceptance, order or delivery.
5 The trust test

When everyone has good reviews, homeowners start guessing.

Another homeowner asked how to choose among four roofers when all four had strong Google and Yelp reviews. They had already been told the installer mattered more than small differences between comparable shingles.

This is the new trust problem. Reviews get you into the final four. Proof of how you work gets you chosen.

PROOF 01

Name the crew lead

Show who supervises the install and who the homeowner calls on job day.

PROOF 02

Show local work

Use real roof photos with the area, material and completion month.

PROOF 03

Explain the handoff

Spell out inspection, cleanup, photos, warranty and final sign-off.

You cannot make this up

The roofer who won the job did not have a seven-touch nurture sequence. He had a phone and arrived.
